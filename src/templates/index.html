Every tab's results (country map, rivers, states, terrain, provinces, strategic regions...) are kept in map_tool/cache/results/, so reopening the tool or coming back to a view doesn't compute them again. A result is reused only while nothing that went into it changed (settings, the tool's code, the downloaded data); the oldest are dropped beyond the size limit. Clearing is never needed for correctness — only to free disk space.

Hidden countries

Still land: unowned on this map, but real coastline, heightmap, terrain and provinces — so a strait stays a strait. The mod turns them into impassable wasteland owned by the country they border most.

Removed countries

Taken off the map entirely: their land becomes sea on every tab, as if they had never been there. Use this rather than Hide when a neighbour is simply in the way (mainland Europe on a British Isles map). A removed country is also dropped from the selection, and the frame no longer counts it. One limit: a neighbour's border with a removed country is a land border in GADM, so that newly exposed shore is not seen as coastline by strait fixing or island separation.
Rivers are added, highest discharge first, until a continent's rivers are this many pixels apart on average (its land area in view divided by its river length). In pixels, so a world map automatically gets far fewer rivers than a Europe map, and a river-dense continent (South America) a lower percentage than Europe. 70 px is about what 5% gives for Europe on a Europe-wide map. It's an average over the whole continent, so its river-rich parts stay denser than its dry ones.
For each continent, only this share of its HydroRIVERS segments in the view with the highest average discharge is drawn (plus everything downstream of them, so every river runs on to its mouth). Lower it for wide views. Continents are HydroSHEDS drainage regions, so a whole river basin shares one value: “Europe” includes Turkey, the Caucasus, Iran, Arabia and the Caspian basin; Oceania includes Indonesia and the Philippines.
Across lakes: the Nile isn't cut by Lake Nasser, nor the Dnieper by its reservoirs. Mouths: where the map's coastline lies beyond the river data's (deltas, estuaries, polders), the river is continued to the nearest sea or to a river that reaches it. Dead ends: rivers ending in the desert or any inland sink other than a lake (Arabian and Saharan wadis, the Tarim) are left out before the percentages are computed. River borders (river_borders.json: the Rhine, Danube, Rio Grande, Mekong, ...): the river is moved onto the border, and where the river rules keep it off, the border is moved to the river instead; the river always stays on one side of it. The fitted country map is downloaded below — use it with rivers.bmp.
A river is a branch from its source to where it joins another river, or a whole river on its own. Branches reaching the image edge and the lower course of a river at its mouth are kept. 0 keeps everything.
A river network is a whole basin: every tributary that touches it, drawn as one connected set of pixels. The game walks each network when it loads the map, at a cost that grows far faster than the network does, and our maps are 20–30× finer than the game's own, so a basin like the Danube fuses into one river the size of the map — measured on a 4096×3072 Yugoslavia map, 623 branches in one river, the game never finished loading in 6 minutes (60 s for the same map with no rivers at all). Vanilla's biggest river has 26 branches and spans an eighth of the map. Over this many branch points, the network is cut into several at its smallest confluences — the fewest cuts that do it, one pixel each, the gap hidden where a tributary meets its parent, and each piece gets its own green source. Measured: 50 → 20 cuts, 140 s to load; 24 → 43 cuts, 65 s. 0 turns the limit off (the map may then take minutes to load, or never finish).

Relief
Heights from the Heightmap tab, with its “exaggerate lower mountain ranges” factor (default thresholds set with ×4). Height differences, never the height itself: highest minus lowest point within the distance (like Paint.NET's Outline effect). A high flat plateau stays flat. Small distance = granular, detailed relief. Smoothing 1 = off.
Forest & farmland
Forest and farmland are mixed with plains in the proportions of the example map. Patch size 0 = pixel speckle. Tropical evergreen forest (within 25° of the equator) is jungle. In trees.bmp, jungle within the edge width of the jungle's border becomes sparse jungle (the blend, 0 = off). Tree density: trees.bmp covers that % of the land cover's forest area (100 = all of it); the thinnest forest is left out first.
Desert & cold
The land cover's bare / sparse vegetation is hot desert below the cold latitude, pixel by pixel: sand with ~35% rocky patches (smoothing = patch size); hills standing on it become desert hills (493B0F, outlined in AE00FF) and mountains desert mountains (F3C793) where bare ground makes up at least a quarter of the land within 10 km (so bare rock in the Alps or the Caucasus stays normal mountains). Beyond the cold latitude it's snowy plains. Transition: grass and shrubs near hot desert get speckled FF00F0, denser where desert covers more of the surroundings within that distance (0 = off). Much of Central Asia (Betpak-Dala, Moyynkum) is shrubland in the land cover, so it stays plains with the transition only near bare ground.
Water & wetlands
Sea and lakes come from the country map. Lakes too small for the Land/Sea step become marsh, with the land cover's wetlands.
